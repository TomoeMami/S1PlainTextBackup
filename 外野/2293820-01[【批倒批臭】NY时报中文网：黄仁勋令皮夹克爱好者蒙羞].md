即使是脸皮再厚的人，这个星期也不好过。

英伟达联合创始人黄仁勋承认与一名被美国情报部门视为间谍的女性有染，这一消息不仅震动了硅谷，也撼动了地缘政治格局。对于穿皮夹克的人群来说，这同样无异于一场地震。

我们不禁要问：黄仁勋之所以如此轻易地落入圈套，是否与他钟爱那些兼具高级时装气质和摩托车风格的夹克有关？这种衣服传递着双重信号：一方面是 “我才不在乎别人怎么看我！”，另一方面又是 “我很可靠，我妻子帮我买所有衣服”。

黄仁勋把这套叙事玩到了极致。他反复告诉全世界，自己的每一件标志性黑皮夹克，都是妻子洛莉挑选的。自从 17 岁和洛莉相识之后，他就不再自己买衣服。他的皮衣，成了他专一顾家、忠于婚姻的象征。其中一件皮衣，甚至在苏富比拍卖会上拍出了 96 万美元的天价。

这就是问题所在：皮夹克本应是反叛者、独行侠、拒绝被世俗规训之人的战袍。它是一种野性的宣言，不适合用来包装 “模范丈夫” 人设。你不能一边披着这件象征不羁的外衣，一边拿它来证明自己是忠诚顾家的好男人。

皮夹克爱好者群体，长久以来都在默默承受世人的偏见。很多人会下意识觉得，穿皮夹克的男人，要么是想装酷，要么正处在中年危机里。我们一直在努力反驳这种刻板印象。

尽管没有得到任何授权 —— 毕竟，征求授权有违我们叛逆不羁的天性 —— 我还是要代表皮夹克爱好者群体发声。我们是早已过气的音乐人，是只想重新找回一点生活激情的父亲，是希望别人以为自己拥有一辆杜卡迪摩托车的人。今天，我们站出来，就是要捍卫皮夹克的名声与荣誉。

我们郑重声明：一个男人在肉 欲上的失足，不应玷污我们的第二层皮肤。

黄仁勋滥用了皮夹克的力量。他把这件野性的外套，变成了用来营销自己忠贞人设的道具。他用皮衣编织谎言，让一件本用来表达自由的服饰，沦为虚伪的包装。

我不是在说所有穿皮夹克的人都忠诚可靠；也不是说穿皮衣就一定花心。服饰不应该为穿戴者的道德负责。但黄仁勋的特殊之处在于，他主动将皮夹克和自己的婚姻忠诚绑定在一起，把皮衣变成他 “好男人” 故事的核心道具。当这个故事崩塌，皮衣的信誉也跟着受损。

以后，当我们这些普通人穿上皮夹克出门，别人看向我们的眼神都会带上一层怀疑：你这件皮衣，也是你妻子买的吗？

这才是最伤人的地方。皮夹克的魅力，恰恰在于它不需要附加任何婚姻证明。它不需要向任何人证明自己的专一。它只需要告诉世界：我选择成为这样的自己。

黄仁勋偷走了这份纯粹。他把皮夹克变成了一种骗局的配饰。

我理解，很多人会觉得这篇文章小题大做。比起芯片供应链、国家安全、硅谷巨头的治理危机，一件皮衣的象征意义，似乎不值一提。但符号是有重量的。当一个文化符号被挪用、被玷污，它会改变所有人使用它的意义。

皮夹克不该是用来打造爱家男人人设的工具。它可以是温柔之人的铠甲，也可以是叛逆者的战袍，但绝不能是谎言的背景板。

所以，致黄仁勋：请你放过皮夹克。

By Anand Giridharadas

Even the thickest skin is having a rough week.

The news that Jensen Huang, the co-founder of Nvidia, had an entanglement with a woman whom American intelligence officials regard as a spy has shaken Silicon Valley and geopolitics. It has also sent tremors through the leather-jacket community.

One has to ask: Was Huang’s vulnerability to entrapment related to his signature garment, those jackets that blend high fashion and biker flair? They send a dual signal: “I don’t care what you think!” and “I am dependable, my wife buys all my clothes.”

Huang mastered this narrative. He told the world repeatedly that each of his iconic black leather jackets was picked out by his wife, Lori. He had stopped buying his own clothes after meeting Lori at age 17. His leather jackets became symbols of his devotion and marital fidelity. One even sold for $960,000 at Sotheby’s.

And that’s the problem. A leather jacket is supposed to be the armor of the rebel, the loner, the person who refuses to be boxed in by convention. It is a wild declaration. It is not meant to be the centerpiece of a model-husband brand. You cannot drape yourself in the garment of nonconformity and wield it as proof that you are a steady family man.

Leather-jacket wearers have long endured prejudice. Many people assume that a man in a leather jacket is either performing coolness or midlife crisis. We have worked against this stereotype.

Without authorization — seeking authorization would be against our rebellious ethos — I speak for the leather-jacket community. We are washed-up musicians, fathers trying to recover a spark, people who want others to assume they own a Ducati. Today we rise to defend the honor of the leather jacket.

We state clearly: A man’s sexual missteps should not taint our second skin.

Jensen Huang misused the power of the leather jacket. He took this feral coat and turned it into a prop for marketing his fidelity. He wove a lie around it, taking a garment of freedom and making it a veneer of hypocrisy.

I am not saying everyone who wears a leather jacket is faithful. Nor am I saying the jacket makes you unfaithful. Clothing should not bear moral responsibility for the wearer. But what makes Huang special is that he voluntarily tied the leather jacket to his marital loyalty, making it central to his story as a good man. When that story collapsed, the jacket’s credibility collapsed with it.

From now on, when the rest of us put on our leather jackets and head out into the world, people will look at us sideways: Did your wife pick this out too?

That is the sting. The magic of the leather jacket is that it needs no marital attestation. It does not have to prove its devotion to anyone. It only has to announce: This is who I choose to be.

Jensen Huang stole that purity. He turned the leather jacket into an accessory of a con.

I get it. Many people will say this essay is trivial. Next to chip supply chains, national security, the governance crisis of a tech titan, the symbolism of a jacket seems small. But symbols carry weight. When a cultural symbol is appropriated and sullied, it changes what it means for everyone who uses it.

The leather jacket was never meant to be a tool for building a brand as a family man. It can be armor for a gentle soul, regalia for a rebel, but never backdrop for a lie.

So to Jensen Huang: Leave the leather jacket alone.

To fellow leather-jacket people: Our jackets are not the problem. The problem is the man who turned them into a prop for loyalty marketing. We will keep wearing them, but we have to clean up the mess Jensen Huang left behind.</blockquote>
