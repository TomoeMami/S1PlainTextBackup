The Myers–Briggs Type Indicator (MBTI) is a self-report questionnaire that makes pseudoscientific claims[6] to categorize individuals into 16 distinct psychological types (often called personality types). The test assigns a binary letter value to each of four dichotomous categories: introversion or extraversion, sensing or intuition, thinking or feeling, and judging or perceiving. This produces a four-letter test result, such as "INTJ" or "ESFP", representing one of the 16 types.[7][8]

迈尔斯-布里格斯类型指标（MBTI）是一种自我报告问卷，它声称能够将人们划分为 16 种不同的心理类型（通常也被称为人格类型）。该测试为每个四个二元类别分配一个字母值：内向或外向、感知型或直觉型、思考型或感受型、判断型或感知型。这样就能得到四个字母的结果，例如“INTJ”或“ESFP”，分别代表这 16 种类型中的一种。 [7] [8]

The original version of the MBTI was constructed during World War II by Americans Katharine Cook Briggs and her daughter Isabel Briggs Myers, inspired by Swiss psychiatrist Carl Jung's 1921 book Psychological Types.[9] Isabel Myers was particularly fascinated by the concept of "introversion", and she typed herself as an INFP. However, she thought that the book was too complex for the general public and tried to organize the Jungian cognitive functions to make it more accessible.[10][failed verification]

MBTI 理论的原始版本是由美国学者凯瑟琳·库克·布里格斯和她的女儿伊莎贝尔·布里格斯·迈尔斯在第二次世界大战期间提出的。这一理论灵感来源于瑞士心理学家卡尔·荣格在 1921 年出版的《心理类型》一书。伊莎贝尔·迈尔斯对“内向”这一概念非常感兴趣，因此她将自己归类为 INFP 类型。不过，她认为荣格的理论过于复杂，难以被大众理解。因此，她试图对荣格的认知功能理论进行整理，使其更易于理解。

As a psychometric indicator, the test exhibits significant deficiencies, including poor validity, poor reliability, measuring supposedly dichotomous categories that are not independent, and not being comprehensive.[11][12][13][14] Most of the research supporting the MBTI's validity has been produced by the Center for Applications of Psychological Type, an organization run by the Myers–Briggs Foundation, and published in the center's own journal, the Journal of Psychological Type (JPT), raising questions of independence, bias and conflict of interest.[15] Nevertheless, due to the Barnum effect, participants often attribute a level of accuracy to the results that is disproportionate to their scientific credibility.[16]

作为一项心理测量指标，这项测试存在显著缺陷：有效性不足，可靠性也较差；所测量的结果实际上属于二元对立的分类，而这些分类并非相互独立；此外，该测试并不具有全面性。支持 MBTI 有效性的大部分研究都是由迈尔斯-布里格斯基金会下属的“心理类型应用中心”所进行的，这些研究发表在该中心的期刊《心理类型杂志》上。这引发了关于其独立性、偏见以及利益冲突的问题。不过，由于巴纳姆效应，参与者往往赋予该测试结果过高的准确性，而这与其科学可信度并不相符。

The MBTI is not considered useful in psychological practice or study, since it lacks predictive power.[17][18] According to University of Pennsylvania professor Adam Grant, "There is no evidence behind it. The traits measured by the test have almost no predictive power when it comes to how happy you'll be in a given situation, how well you'll perform at your job, or how satisfied you'll be in your marriage."[19] Despite controversies over validity, the instrument has demonstrated widespread influence since its adoption by the Educational Testing Service in 1962. It is estimated that 50 million people have taken the MBTI and that 10,000 businesses, 2,500 colleges and universities, and 200 government agencies in the United States use it.[20]

MBTI 在心理学实践或研究中并不被认为具有实用性，因为它缺乏预测能力。根据宾夕法尼亚大学教授亚当·格兰特的观点：“没有证据表明这种测试能够准确预测一个人在特定情境下的幸福感、工作表现或婚姻满意度。”尽管存在关于其有效性的争议，但自 1962 年被教育测试服务局采用以来，这种工具已经产生了广泛的影响。据估计，有 5000 万人参加过 MBTI 测试，而美国有 10,000 家企业、2,500 所大学和 200 个政府机构使用这种工具进行评估。
