A fundamental question that had to be faced by the metric conversion committees within Air Canada was how to implement the decision to move towards metrification. It was decided to do it by aircraft type. So it was that the 767 was introduced as a metric aircraft. On the other hand, a new version of the Lockheed L-1011, the Lockheed L-1011-500, acquired at about the same time, was not introduced as a metric aircraft. It was not considered safe to mix metric and non-metric measurements within the same classification or type of aircraft.¹²⁴

After Gimli, another fundamental question had to be decided, namely, whether to revert to Imperial measurements in the case of the 767. An alternative question, equally fundamental, was whether to speed up the process of metrification within the whole fleet and, in particular, to move towards standardization of fuel gauges and drip sticks in metric measurements.

The Fuel Committee established after Gimli recommended against reconversion of the 767 to Imperial measurements. The basis of this recommendation was their finding that the cause of the accident was not a metric mix-up. According to Air Canada, the miscalculation of the fuel weight arose basically from a failure within Air Canada to allocate responsibility for making the mathematical computation from litres to kilograms in the abnormal situation which arose. In a normal situation, with the fuel gauges operating and the flight management control system working, the conversion would have been done automatically within the computerized system.

In the circumstances under consideration, an attempt was made by the personnel involved to do the calculations themselves. Without the aid of the computerized system, which was not working, the wrong conversion factor was used and the fuel load miscalculated. The Fuel Committee came to the conclusion that the process of making the computation was not defined in the manuals.¹²⁵

As noted above, <strong>when using the drip procedure for calculating fuel weight, different conversions are made, depending on the type of aircraft. Conversions are made not only as between Imperial and metric, but also conversions using the U.S. gallon which is different in size from the Imperial gallon.</strong> What is important in all these cases is to use the right number, that is, the right conversion factor.

加拿大航空内部负责公制化转换的委员会必须面对的一个根本问题是，如何落实向公制计量体系转变的决定。

最终决定按照飞机型号来实施。因此，波音767被作为一款采用公制计量的飞机引入。

另一方面，大约在同一时期购入的新型号洛克希德L-1011，即洛克希德L-1011-500，并没有作为公制飞机引入。

人们认为，在同一类别或同一型号的飞机中混用公制和非公制计量单位是不安全的。

Gimli事故（1985年吉姆利滑翔机事故）发生后，还必须决定另一个根本性问题，即波音767是否应该重新改用英制计量单位。

另一个同样根本的问题是，是否应该加快整个机队的公制化进程，尤其是推动燃油量表和测油尺统一采用公制计量单位。

吉姆利事故之后成立的燃油委员会建议，不要将波音767重新改回英制计量单位。

这一建议的依据是，他们认定事故原因并不是公制计量单位的混淆。

根据加拿大航空的说法，燃油重量计算错误基本上源于加拿大航空内部未能明确分配责任，即在当时出现的异常情况下，没有明确由谁负责将升换算为千克的数学计算工作。

在正常情况下，如果燃油量表正常工作，并且飞行管理控制系统运行正常，那么这一换算工作本应由计算机系统自动完成。

在当时的情况下，相关工作人员试图自行进行计算。

由于计算机系统没有正常工作，他们在没有计算机系统辅助的情况下，使用了错误的换算系数，从而错误地计算了燃油装载量。

燃油委员会最终得出的结论是，相关手册中并没有明确规定这一计算过程。

如上所述，<strong>在使用测油尺程序计算燃油重量时，根据飞机型号的不同，需要进行不同的单位换算。</strong>
<strong>这些换算不仅包括英制单位与公制单位之间的转换，还包括使用美制加仑进行的换算，因为美制加仑与英制加仑的容量不同</strong>。

在所有这些情况下，重要的是使用正确的数值，也就是正确的换算系数。
